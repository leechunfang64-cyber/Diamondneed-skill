# Competitor Analysis Rubric

Use this rubric to judge whether existing competitors are strong, weak, beatable, or dangerous.

The goal is not to admire competitors. The goal is to answer:

> Can a small team beat this market with better product, better content, better UI, better private-domain conversion, better automation, or better delivery?

## 1. Competitor Types

### 1.1 Direct Competitors

Products solving the same problem for the same user.

Examples:

- Same AI tool category
- Same SaaS workflow
- Same virtual product
- Same template product
- Same Xiaohongshu item

### 1.2 Indirect Competitors

Products solving part of the same problem.

Examples:

- General-purpose AI tools
- Notion templates
- Prompt packs
- Excel / spreadsheet workflows
- Manual service providers
- Plugin extensions

### 1.3 Manual Substitutes

Users are already solving the problem manually.

Examples:

- freelancers
- agencies
- assistants
- teachers
- editors
- designers
-运营人员
- scripts
- spreadsheets
- copy-paste workflows

Manual substitutes are important because they often reveal productization opportunities.

### 1.4 Platform Substitutes

A large platform may already provide part of the solution.

Examples:

- Canva
- CapCut
- ChatGPT
- Claude
- Notion
- Xiaohongshu native features
- TikTok / Douyin creator tools
- Google Workspace
- Microsoft Office

Platform substitutes are dangerous if they can easily absorb the feature.

### 1.5 Informal Sellers

Non-standard sellers who prove payment demand.

Examples:

- Xiaohongshu sellers
- Xianyu sellers
- Pinduoduo sellers
- Taobao sellers
- Fiverr gigs
- Upwork services
- Telegram / Discord sellers
- WeChat private-domain sellers

Informal sellers are often weak competitors but strong demand signals.

## 2. Competitor Data To Collect

For each competitor, collect:

- Name
- URL / platform
- Type
- Target user
- Core offer
- Pricing
- Main traffic channel
- Recent activity
- Sales/order/review signal
- Product quality
- UI/UX quality
- Content quality
- Customer service quality
- Private-domain / community strategy
- SEO strength
- Social media strength
- Brand strength
- Product moat
- Traffic moat
- User complaints
- Weaknesses
- How we can beat them

Use this table:

| Competitor | Type | Offer | Pricing | Traffic | Sales Signal | Strength | Weakness | Can Beat? | Attack Angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 3. Competitor Strength Score

Score each competitor from 0 to 5 in the following dimensions.

### 3.1 Product Quality

```text
0 = broken or unclear product
1 = very rough, barely usable
2 = usable but incomplete
3 = acceptable product
4 = polished and mature
5 = excellent product with strong workflow and retention
```

### 3.2 UI / UX Quality

```text
0 = confusing, ugly, hard to use
1 = rough and amateur
2 = usable but unattractive
3 = decent
4 = polished
5 = premium, smooth, high trust
```

### 3.3 Content / Marketing Quality

```text
0 = almost no marketing
1 = amateur content, weak copywriting
2 = some content but inconsistent
3 = decent content and positioning
4 = strong content system
5 = professional content matrix, strong conversion, clear brand
```

### 3.4 Traffic Moat

```text
0 = no visible traffic moat
1 = occasional content or weak traffic
2 = some SEO/social traffic
3 = stable traffic source
4 = strong SEO, content matrix, community, or private domain
5 = dominant search/social/platform traffic advantage
```

### 3.5 Brand / Trust

```text
0 = no trust
1 = low trust, random seller
2 = some trust signals
3 = normal trust
4 = strong brand or reputation
5 = category leader or platform-level trust
```

### 3.6 Product Moat

```text
0 = no moat
1 = simple feature, easy to copy
2 = basic workflow, still easy to copy
3 = some data, template, integration, or workflow moat
4 = strong workflow, user lock-in, data, or distribution moat
5 = hard technical, data, network, or ecosystem moat
```

### 3.7 Sales / Paid Validation

```text
0 = no paid signal
1 = weak interest only
2 = some paid traces
3 = stable paid behavior
4 = strong order/revenue signal
5 = repeated purchase, subscription, or category-level paid demand
```

### 3.8 Support / Delivery Quality

```text
0 = no delivery clarity
1 = poor support, many complaints
2 = basic support
3 = acceptable support
4 = professional support and delivery
5 = strong SOP, automation, customer success, and retention
```

## 4. Small-Team Attackability Score

This is the most important score.

Ask:

> Can we realistically beat this competitor with our resources?

Score from 0 to 5:

```text
0 = cannot beat; avoid direct competition
1 = very hard; only possible with strong niche angle
2 = possible but costly
3 = beatable with focused execution
4 = easy to beat with better product/content/UX/conversion
5 = obvious weak opponent; strong chance for 降维打击
```

A competitor can be high revenue but still attackable if:

- Their UI is ugly.
- Their content is weak.
- Their customer service is poor.
- Their product is incomplete.
- Their pricing is confusing.
- Their traffic is accidental.
- Their reviews expose clear pain.
- They lack private-domain retention.
- They lack automation.
- They serve too broad a market and ignore a profitable niche.

A competitor can be low revenue but dangerous if:

- They are professional but still cannot sell.
- They have tested the market and found weak demand.
- They have hidden cost or legal issues.
- The user need looks interesting but does not convert.

## 5. Competitor Opportunity Quadrant

### A. Weak Competitors + Strong Demand

Best opportunity.

Signals:

- Competitors have sales.
- Competitors are unprofessional.
- Users still buy despite bad UX/content/service.
- There are visible complaints.
- There is room for better productization.

Decision:

> Priority research or Small Bet / Go Now.

### B. Strong Competitors + Strong Demand

Validated but competitive.

Signals:

- Multiple strong products.
- Strong SEO or content moat.
- Clear paid demand.
- Category is mature.

Decision:

> Do not copy directly. Find niche, underserved segment, cheaper workflow, higher-end service, or new channel.

### C. Weak Competitors + Weak Demand

Probably not a blue ocean.

Signals:

- Few sellers.
- Weak content.
- Weak sales.
- Few users ask to buy.
- Search and social demand are weak.

Decision:

> Observe or reject unless other evidence appears.

### D. Strong Competitors + Weak Demand

Usually bad.

Signals:

- Competitors look professional.
- But sales, traffic, and engagement are weak.
- Users are not paying.

Decision:

> Reject or only watch.

## 6. Attack Angles

When competitors are beatable, identify the exact attack angle.

Possible attack angles:

### Product Attack

- simpler workflow
- better output quality
- faster generation
- fewer bugs
- better templates
- better onboarding
- better mobile experience
- better Chinese localization
- better niche-specific workflow

### Content Attack

- more precise Xiaohongshu notes
- better titles and covers
- more real use cases
- stronger before/after demos
- tutorial-style content
- comparison content
- case-study content
- SEO landing pages

### Conversion Attack

- clearer pricing
- better landing page
- stronger CTA
- trust signals
- examples and demos
- trial offer
- better customer support
- private-domain follow-up

### Delivery Attack

- automatic delivery
- faster fulfillment
- lower support burden
- better SOP
- better refund handling
- better post-purchase guide

### Business Model Attack

- lower price with automation
- higher price with service
- subscription
- one-time package
- credits
- VIP bundle
- affiliate / reseller
- private-domain repurchase

### Niche Attack

- narrower user group
- narrower use case
- industry-specific version
- platform-specific version
- language-specific version
- beginner-specific version
- professional/high-end version

## 8. Hard Truth

A market is attractive only when both sides are true:

```text
users want it enough to pay
competitors are weak enough for us to win
```

Do not enter a market just because demand exists.

Do not enter a market just because competitors are weak.

The best market is:

```text
strong paid demand + weak current supply + reachable traffic + realistic execution path
```
