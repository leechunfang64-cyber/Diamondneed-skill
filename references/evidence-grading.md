# Evidence Grading

Use this reference to grade opportunity evidence. The goal is to avoid confusing attention with demand and demand with payment.

## 1. Four Signal Levels

### 1.1 Interest Signal

Means users noticed it. It does not prove willingness to pay.

Examples:

- Views
- Likes
- Saves
- Shares
- Generic comments such as "不错", "想要", "mark"
- Free trial signups without usage or payment
- Tool directory listing
- Social discussion without purchase path

How to use:

- Good for early discovery.
- Weak for go/no-go decisions.
- Never treat this as paid validation.

### 1.2 Demand Signal

Means users repeatedly show a real problem or active search behavior.

Examples:

- Search volume or search suggestions with clear intent
- Repeated questions in comments, forums, groups, Reddit, Zhihu, Xiaohongshu
- Users describe a concrete pain
- Users share manual workarounds
- Users complain about existing products
- Users ask for alternatives
- Multiple creators or sellers talk about the same need

How to use:

- Demand is stronger when the scenario is specific, frequent, urgent, or tied to making/saving money.
- Demand is weaker when it is only curiosity, trend-chasing, or entertainment.

### 1.3 Payment Signal

Means money probably changed hands.

Examples:

- Store order count
- Paid reviews
- Paid user screenshots
- Marketplace sales
- Service orders
- Subscription pricing with visible paying users
- Comments asking "怎么买", "多少钱", "链接在哪里" plus seller reply or purchase path
- Users discuss delivery, refund, account, invoice, usage limit, renewal, or after-sales

How to use:

- Payment signal is the main reason to move from research to MVP.
- Grade payment quality by order speed, price, margin, refund risk, support burden, and traffic source.

### 1.4 Retention Signal

Means the opportunity may last beyond one transaction.

Examples:

- Renewal
- Subscription
- Repeat purchase
- Users saving templates/workflows over time
- Private-domain community with repeated purchases
- Users recommending it after use
- Multiple use cases around the same core product

How to use:

- Retention signal raises confidence and revenue quality.
- One-time viral orders without repeat purchase should be discounted.

## 2. Paid Validation Levels

| Level | Meaning | Typical Evidence | Decision Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak | Interest only | Likes, saves, views, comments, free users | Do not build full product |
| Medium | Early payment | A few orders, paid reviews, small marketplace sales | Run Small Bet |
| Strong | Repeatable payment | Weekly/monthly orders, clear price, acceptable margin | Build focused MVP |
| Very Strong | Scaled and repeatable | Large order signal, multiple sellers, repeat purchase, clear channel | Go Now if risk is controlled |

## 3. Order Velocity

Order count means little without time.

| Signal | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 24h orders | Strong burst, but check ads, fake orders, or one-off viral effect |
| 48h orders | Good early signal if traffic source is clear |
| 1 week orders | Stronger than one-day spike |
| 1 month orders | Better for judging stability |
| 3+ months orders | Good for durability, but check growth and margin |

## 4. Traffic Quality

| Traffic Type | Quality | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Broad curiosity traffic | Low | Example: "free ai tools"; usually weak conversion |
| Problem-aware traffic | Medium | User knows the pain but may not know solution |
| Solution-aware traffic | High | User is comparing tools/services |
| Purchase-intent traffic | Very high | User searches price, coupon, buy, template, service, plugin |
| Private-domain repeat traffic | High | Strong if trust and conversion path exist |

## 5. Competitor Weakness Signals

A competitor is truly weak only if users still buy despite obvious flaws.

Strong opportunity signals:

- Page is rough but has orders
- Customer service is slow but users still ask to buy
- Product is narrow but solves one painful job
- Seller has poor content but still sells
- Multiple amateur sellers all get orders

False weakness signals:

- Site looks ugly but has strong SEO
- Brand looks small but has private-domain traffic
- Product looks simple but has hard data, trust, or supply-chain moat
- Competitor has poor UI but users do not care because the job is urgent

## 6. Red Flags

Downgrade confidence when:

- No paid path is visible
- Users ask only for free options
- Comments are generic praise, not buying intent
- Orders are likely fake, boosted, or from one viral post
- Legal or platform risk is core to the product
- API or delivery cost may destroy margin
- Support burden is high
- The user cannot personally understand the scenario
- The channel is broad but not precise
- No compounding asset is created after work is done
